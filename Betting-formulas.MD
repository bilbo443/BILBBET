# Bilbbet Betting Formulas -- Reference Report

Everything below was extracted directly from the current, live code (both
the client-side app and the Python pipeline) rather than recalled from
memory, specifically so this reflects what actually runs, not an
approximation of it. Special betting categories (weekly winner, charity,
philanthropy, novelty) are excluded per request -- those are simpler,
fixed-outcome markets without a simulation behind them.

## The shared foundation every market builds on

Every market in this platform -- H2H, division futures, promotion, Roddy,
FA Cup -- ultimately comes down to the same core technique: **simulate a
team's likely score by drawing randomly from a pool of plausible values**,
repeated thousands of times, then count outcomes. The specific pool and
adjustments differ per market, but the mechanism doesn't.

### The sampling pool

For a given team, the pool of possible scores is built from:

1. **The team's own historical scores** (`H2H_HISTORY` / `history`), if
   any exist.
2. **A "shift"** -- a single number added to every sampled value,
   representing how much stronger or weaker this team is expected to be
   than its own history suggests. Comes from a pre-computed coefficient
   (different per market -- see below), and can be nudged further by
   actual in-season results once some exist (see "Live-season blending"
   below).
3. **Variance widening** -- for a team with little or no track record, a
   configurable fraction of samples are drawn from a *wider* reference
   pool (division-wide or league-wide) instead of just their own thin
   history. This widens the spread of outcomes rather than collapsing
   toward a single average, on the reasoning that a new/promoted manager
   plausibly falls into one of two very different groups (genuinely
   competitive, or disengaged after initial setup) rather than clustering
   near a mean.

A single simulated "score" for a team is then just: pick one value at
random from this pool.

### Live-season blending

Once real results exist for a team this season, the shift used isn't just
the static, pre-season coefficient -- it's nudged toward what the team has
actually been scoring, weighted by recency (recent rounds count for more
than early-season ones) and by how many rounds have actually been played
(more real data = more trust placed in it, up to a capped confidence
level). A team with zero real results this season still uses the original,
static coefficient unchanged.

### From simulation to a percentage to odds

Every market runs many thousands of full simulated trials (the exact count
varies by market, noted below), counts how often a given outcome occurred,
and converts that count into a percentage. That percentage is converted to
decimal odds as:

```
odds = 1 / (probability x 1.05)
```

The `x 1.05` is a 5% built-in margin (the house edge) -- applied
uniformly, not varied per market or per team. Odds are floored at 1.005
and capped at 1001; anything that would price below roughly 1.0025
probability is marked suspended rather than shown as a real price.

## H2H markets

Computed entirely client-side (`computeH2HMarket`), on demand, not
pre-generated by the pipeline.

**Simulation size**: 20,000 trials per matchup by default (some call
sites request fewer for performance-sensitive contexts like the featured
picks list).

**Process**: draw 20,000 simulated scores for each team independently from
their sampling pool (division-context coefficient, called `eliza` in the
underlying data), compare each pair of draws.

- **Moneyline** (`aWinPct`/`bWinPct`/`drawPct`): straightforward count of
  how often each team's simulated score was higher, lower, or equal.
- **Handicap line**: the average margin across all 20,000 trials, floored
  to the nearest whole number then offset by 0.5 (guaranteeing no possible
  push/draw on the line itself). Each side's "cover" percentage is how
  often their actual simulated margin beat that line.
- **Head-to-head history edge**: if two specific teams have a meaningful
  head-to-head record against each other (minimum games played and a
  minimum win-margin threshold, both configurable), the team with the
  historical edge gets a flat point bonus added to every one of their
  simulated draws for this matchup specifically -- decayed by how long ago
  that head-to-head history was established, and capped at a maximum
  bonus size.
- **"Viable range"** shown per team: the 25th-75th percentile band of that
  team's own simulated scores -- shown instead of a single average so a
  volatile team's spread is visible, not flattened into one number.

## How a team's coefficient is actually determined

Every market above leans on a per-team coefficient (`eliza`, `roddy`,
`fa_cup`/`ecl`, `relegation_risk`, `variance_widen`) without explaining
where those numbers come from. Traced directly through
`rebuild_coefficients.py`, the script that actually produces them.

### The raw input: four seasons of history

Four historical season files (`22/23` through `25/26`), each mapping a
team to their division that season and their round-by-round scores.
Team renames are resolved first via an alias map (built from the admin
roster's `prev_names` field), so a team that changed names doesn't get
split into two separate histories.

### Step 1 -- per-season, per-team metrics

For each season a team has data in, several things get computed:

- **`roddy_z`**: the team's average score that season, z-scored against
  the *entire league's* mean and standard deviation that season --
  tier-blind, since Roddy and the knockout cups aren't division-relative
  competitions.
- **`eliza_z`**: the team's average score z-scored against their *own
  tier's* mean and standard deviation, then offset by the gap between
  that tier's mean and the team's *current* division's tier mean (both
  relative to the league-wide spread) -- so a team's division-relative
  strength this season is expressed in terms comparable to whatever
  division they're actually in now, not the one they played in
  historically. A modest strength-of-schedule adjustment is added on
  top where that data exists (weighted low -- it only covers 2 of the 4
  seasons and is treated as a refinement, not a replacement for the tier
  adjustment).
- **First-half / second-half z-scores**: the same tier-relative z-score,
  computed separately for rounds 1-13 and 14+ that season -- feeds into
  "trajectory" below.
- **`ceiling_z`**: for teams with at least 10 rounds played that season,
  their own coefficient of variation (score volatility relative to their
  own mean), z-scored against the league-wide spread of *how volatile
  teams tend to be* that season. Specific to the knockout-cup
  coefficients -- a single-elimination format rewards a high-ceiling day
  more than a season-long average does, and this is confirmed to reflect
  a genuinely separate dimension from raw quality, not just weaker teams
  being more erratic.

### Step 2 -- combining seasons into one profile

- **Recency weighting**: the four seasons are blended using fixed
  weights (`25/26`: 0.4, `24/25`: 0.3, `23/24`: 0.2, `22/23`: 0.1) --
  more recent seasons count for more, but older ones aren't discarded
  entirely.
- **Shrinkage**: the blended value is pulled toward zero by a factor
  that *increases* with how many seasons of data exist (`0.4 + 0.15 x
  n_seasons`, capped at 0.9) -- more history earns more confidence in
  deviating from a neutral, average assumption.
- **Promotion discount**: if a team's *best* historically-played tier is
  higher than their current division, that shrinkage factor gets pulled
  back down further (up to 65% less confidence) -- past success in a
  tougher tier doesn't fully carry over at the same confidence into a
  lower one.
- **Trajectory**: the average of (second-half z minus first-half z)
  across all seasons with both halves available -- whether a team tends
  to finish seasons stronger or weaker than they started. Stored on the
  profile but not currently folded into any live market coefficient.

### Step 3 -- the coefficients actually used

| Coefficient | Derivation |
|---|---|
| `eliza` | Recency-weighted, shrunk `eliza_z` |
| `roddy` | Recency-weighted, shrunk `roddy_z` |
| `fa_cup` / `ecl` | `(roddy_raw + 0.18 x ceiling_raw) x shrink` -- same base strength as Roddy, plus a modest ceiling/volatility boost |
| `relegation_risk` | The most recent season's `eliza_z`, inverted and scaled (`x -0.3`) -- a team trending well gets a *negative* relegation-risk value (i.e. safer) |
| `variance_widen` | `max(0.5 - 0.12 x n_seasons, 0)` -- less established teams get more spread blended into their sampling pool; a team with 4+ seasons of data gets none |

### From coefficient to an actual points shift

Every coefficient above is a unitless z-score, not raw fantasy points.
The conversion is a single, fixed scale constant (`15.0459...`,
currently a hardcoded value rather than something recomputed on every
rebuild) multiplied against the coefficient -- e.g. the shift used for
division-futures markets is `scale x (eliza - 0.5 x relegation_risk)`,
and the FA Cup/ECL shift is `scale x fa_cup`. This is the number added
to every one of a team's simulated score draws, described in the
"shared foundation" section above.

### Live-season blending, revisited

The recency-weighted in-season blending described earlier
(`compute_adjusted_shifts`) sits on top of all of this -- it doesn't
replace the historical coefficient, it nudges the *shift* derived from
it, in proportion to how many real rounds have actually been played
this season (capped at 50% confidence even with a full season of live
data, so the historical profile is never fully discarded).

## Division futures (win division / top 3 / top half / bottom half /
relegation / wooden spoon)

Computed by the Python pipeline (`simulate_division_futures`), pre-
generated rather than live per-request.

**Simulation size**: `N_SIM` full simulated seasons (the module-level
constant governing every futures market's trial count).

**Process**: for each of the `N_SIM` trials, simulate an entire double
round-robin season for the division -- every scheduled fixture gets two
random score draws (one per team), 3 points for a win, 1 point each for a
draw, ranked by points then by total score-for as the tiebreaker. Record
each team's finishing position for that trial. After all trials, each
market is just how often a team's finishing position fell within the
relevant range:

| Market | Condition |
|---|---|
| `win_div_pct` | Finished 1st |
| `top3_pct` | Finished in the top 3 |
| `top_half_pct` | Finished in the top half (division size / 2, rounded down) |
| `bottom_half_pct` | Finished in the bottom half |
| `relegation_pct` | Finished in the bottom 4 (Eliza Cup) or bottom 3 (Division 2) |
| `wooden_spoon_pct` | Finished last |

(Division 3's `bottom3_pct` follows the identical "bottom N positions"
logic as `relegation_pct`, just without an actual relegation destination
below it.)

The coefficient driving each team's shift here is specifically the
`eliza` value, with an additional **relegation-risk penalty** subtracted
from it -- a team flagged as a relegation risk gets a further downward
shift on top of their base coefficient, specific to this market.

## Promotion market

Computed by the Python pipeline (`simulate_promotion_market` +
`simulate_promotion_playoffs`). The full, confirmed bracket structure
(seeding, the Elimination-Final/Promotion-Final cross-overs, exact places
per division) is documented in detail in `COMPETITION_RULES.md` -- not
repeated here to avoid the two documents drifting apart. In formula terms:
the same round-robin conference simulation as division futures determines
each conference's finishing order; the top spot(s) auto-promote; the next
4 playoff-eligible finishers per conference feed the confirmed bracket
format, with each simulated match decided by one random draw per team,
higher score wins.

## Roddy

Computed by the Python pipeline (`simulate_roddy_market`). An open-field
ranking across every team in the platform, not split into divisions.

**Simulation size**: 25,000 trials.

**Process**: for each trial, draw a full season's worth of round scores
for every team (using the `roddy` coefficient specifically -- raw scoring
strength, deliberately tier-blind, unlike the division-relative `eliza`
coefficient), sum each team's season total, rank all teams by that total.

| Market | Condition |
|---|---|
| `roddy_win_pct` | Ranked 1st overall |
| `roddy_top3_pct` | Ranked in the top 3 |
| `roddy_top5_pct` | Ranked in the top 5 |
| `roddy_top10_pct` | Ranked in the top 10 |

This is the one futures market that blends in live in-season results
directly (the same recency-weighted blending described above) -- it
previously didn't, which meant it stayed frozen at its pre-season estimate
even as real rounds accumulated for every other market; that inconsistency
has been corrected.

## FA Cup

Computed by the Python pipeline (`simulate_fa_cup_bracket`).

**Simulation size**: 25,000 trials.

**The bracket itself**: fixed once, not re-randomized per trial -- a
single 64-slot draw (2 bye slots straight into the Round of 32, 62 teams
paired into 30 Round-of-64 matches), seeded once and reused for every
trial, the same way a real tournament draw is fixed before it's played
out. What varies between trials is only who wins each individual match,
not who plays whom.

**Process**: each match is decided by one random score draw per team
(using the `fa_cup` coefficient), higher score advances. Recorded per team,
per trial: whether they reached Round of 32, Round of 16, Quarter-Final,
Semi-Final, the Final, and whether they won it outright.

| Market | Bracket stage | Confirmed field size |
|---|---|---|
| `win_pct` | Won the tournament | 1 of 64 |
| `reach_final_pct` | Reached the Final | 2 of 64 |
| `reach_sf_pct` | Reached the Semi-Final | 4 of 64 |
| `reach_qf_pct` | Reached the Quarter-Final | 8 of 64 |
| `reach_r16_pct` | Reached the Round of 16 | 16 of 64 |
| `reach_r32_pct` | Reached the Round of 32 | 32 of 64 |

## ECL

Computed by the Python pipeline (`simulate_ecl_market`) -- previously
placeholder data with no real methodology behind it; built out with the
same rigor as FA Cup, once a confirmed entrant list existed to build
against.

**The field**: a fixed 12 teams (`futures.json`'s existing `ecl_markets`
roster) -- 9 from Eliza Cup, 3 from Division 2 (1 from 2A, 2 from 2B).

**Simulation size**: 25,000 trials.

**Format**: a 3-matchday league phase, then a knockout bracket for the top
8. The league-phase schedule is a round-robin subset (`round_robin_schedule`,
taking just the first 3 rounds) -- fixed once, not re-randomized per
trial, guaranteeing every team plays exactly 3 different opponents with no
repeat matchups. This is a standard, defensible choice, not a replication
of a specific real ECL draw, since no fixed real-world schedule for this
exists to replicate -- the same reasoning already applied to FA Cup's
Round-of-64 draw.

**Process**: each matchday is decided by one random score draw per team
(using the `ecl` coefficient), 3 points for a win, 1 point each for a
draw, ranked by points then total score-for. The top 8 advance to a
standard seeded knockout bracket (1v8, 4v5, 2v7, 3v6 -- protecting the
strongest league-phase finishers from meeting early, the same seeding
intent as the promotion playoff bracket), then Semi-Final -> Final ->
winner, each decided the same way as a league-phase match.

| Market | Stage | Confirmed field size |
|---|---|---|
| `win_pct` | Won the tournament | 1 of 12 |
| `reach_final_pct` | Reached the Final | 2 of 12 |
| `reach_sf_pct` | Reached the Semi-Final | 4 of 12 |
| `reach_knockout_pct` | Finished top 8 in the league phase | 8 of 12 |

**Verified**: the league-phase schedule generator was checked directly --
confirmed zero repeated matchups across the 3 matchdays and every team
playing exactly 3 games. The full simulation was run against real team
data and checked for internal consistency: probabilities summed to
exactly 800% for the knockout cutoff (8 teams), 400% for the
Semi-Final (4), 200% for the Final (2), and 100% for the win market (1)
-- confirming the simulation counts are mathematically sound, not just
plausible-looking.

## Multi-bet combined odds

**Independent picks** (the default): combined odds are the straight
product of each pick's individual odds -- `odds_A x odds_B x ...`.

**"Places" markets specifically** (top 3, top half, bottom half,
relegation, promotion, bottom 3, Roddy's tiered markets, FA Cup's bracket
stages): when a slip picks 2+ different teams from the *same* limited
pool of outcomes (e.g. two different teams both "to finish top 3" in the
same division), straight multiplication overstates the true probability,
since those teams are competing for the same shrinking pool of spots, not
independent events. A hypergeometric-style correction is applied instead:

```
correction factor = (places / total_teams)^n  /  true joint probability
adjusted combined odds = naive combined odds x correction factor
```

where `n` is how many distinct teams were picked from that same pool, and
"true joint probability" is the actual without-replacement probability of
all `n` landing in the available places together. This is a deliberate
approximation (assumes roughly even spacing between teams' individual
chances within one pool, rather than a full joint simulation of the
season) -- accepted as a fair trade-off for a for-fun platform.

ECL is now included in this correction (previously excluded for having no
confirmed entrant count) -- its 12-team field and stage sizes are
verified the same way FA Cup's are.
